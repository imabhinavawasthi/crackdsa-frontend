# Theory & Concepts
The **Maximum Product Subarray** problem requires us to find the contiguous subarray that yields the largest product. It is a tricky variation of the Maximum Subarray (Kadane's Algorithm).

Unlike addition, multiplication behaves non-linearly with negative numbers. A large negative product can suddenly become a massive positive product if multiplied by another negative number! Because of this, we must keep track of **both** the maximum product and the minimum (most negative) product up to the current element.

At any index `i`, the maximum product ending at `i` could be derived from:
1. `max_product_ending_here * nums[i]` (if `nums[i]` is positive)
2. `min_product_ending_here * nums[i]` (if `nums[i]` is negative)
3. `nums[i]` itself (starting a new subarray)

# Common Pitfalls
<Warning>
- **Forgetting the Temporary Variable:** When updating `max_so_far` and `min_so_far`, you must use a `temp_max` variable. If you update `max_so_far` directly and then use it to calculate `min_so_far`, your calculation will be corrupted!
- **Handling Zeroes:** Zeroes effectively "reset" the product. The `max()` and `min()` functions handle this gracefully by picking the single element `nums[i]` (which is `0`) over the multiplication.
</Warning>
