# Theory & Concepts
**Fast & Slow Pointers**, formally known as **Floyd's Cycle-Finding Algorithm** (or the Tortoise and Hare algorithm), is a pointer technique specifically designed to detect cycles in a sequence, usually a Linked List or an Array where values act as pointers.

Unlike other two-pointer techniques that iterate over an array to find pairs or filter elements, this algorithm involves two pointers moving through a sequence at **different speeds**.

### The Core Intuition
Imagine two runners on a track. One runner (the Tortoise) is slow, taking one step at a time. The other runner (the Hare) is fast, taking two steps at a time. 

- **If the track is a straight line:** The Hare will reach the finish line (a `null` pointer) long before the Tortoise.
- **If the track has a loop:** The Hare will never find a finish line. Instead, it will loop around and eventually "lap" the Tortoise. If they ever land on the exact same spot at the exact same time, a cycle definitively exists.

#### Finding the Start of the Cycle
Detecting the cycle is easy. But finding exactly *where* the cycle begins requires a bit of math.
Let:
- $L_1$ be the distance from the head to the start of the cycle.
- $L_2$ be the distance from the start of the cycle to the intersection point where Tortoise and Hare meet.
- $C$ be the total length of the cycle.

When they meet, the Tortoise has traveled $L_1 + L_2$.
The Hare has traveled $L_1 + L_2 + kC$ (where $k$ is the number of times it looped).
Since the Hare travels exactly twice as fast as the Tortoise:
$2(L_1 + L_2) = L_1 + L_2 + kC$
$L_1 + L_2 = kC$
$L_1 = kC - L_2$

This equation tells us something profound: **The distance from the head to the start of the cycle ($L_1$) is equal to the distance from the intersection point to the start of the cycle ($kC - L_2$).** 

Therefore, if we reset the Tortoise back to the head of the list, and slow the Hare down so both now take exactly **one step at a time**, the exact point where they meet again will be the start of the cycle!

# Common Pitfalls
<Warning>
- **Null Reference Exceptions:** Always check `while (fast && fast->next)`. If you only check `fast`, `fast->next->next` will throw an error when `fast->next` is null.
- **Forgetting Phase 2 logic:** Finding the start of the cycle is a common follow-up. Do not forget that after they meet, you must reset ONE pointer to the head, and slow the other pointer down to 1 step at a time.
</Warning>
