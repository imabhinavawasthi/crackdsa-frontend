# Theory & Concepts
Finding the longest palindromic substring is a classic problem that tests your understanding of strings and two-pointer techniques. A palindrome reads the same forwards and backwards.

The naive approach involves checking all possible substrings, leading to an $O(N^3)$ time complexity. However, we can optimize this significantly. The optimal approach is **Expand Around Center**. Since a palindrome mirrors around its center, we can iterate through each character (and the space between characters) in the string, treating it as the center, and expand outward as long as the characters match. This brings the time complexity down to $O(N^2)$ while keeping the space complexity at $O(1)$. 

Another possible approach is Dynamic Programming, but it requires $O(N^2)$ space, making the expand-around-center method strictly better in practice. There's also Manacher's Algorithm, which achieves $O(N)$ time, but it's generally considered overkill for standard interviews unless specifically asked for.

# Common Pitfalls
<Warning>
- **Missing Even-Length Palindromes:** The center of a palindrome can be exactly on a character (odd length like 'aba') or between two characters (even length like 'abba'). Ensure you check both `(i, i)` and `(i, i+1)`.
- **Substring Copying:** Avoid repeatedly slicing or copying substrings in every iteration, as this adds an invisible O(N) overhead. Keep track of indices (`start` and `maxLen`) and only take the substring at the very end.
</Warning>
