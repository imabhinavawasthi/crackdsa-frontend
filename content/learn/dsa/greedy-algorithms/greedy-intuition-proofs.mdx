# Theory & Concepts
**Greedy Intuition & Proofs** focuses on the crucial step of validating a greedy algorithm. Coming up with a greedy strategy is often the easy part; proving its correctness is where the true challenge lies. 

In competitive programming and technical interviews, an unproven greedy strategy is a huge risk. You might pass the sample test cases but fail on hidden edge cases. To confidently use a greedy approach, you must prove that your locally optimal choices lead to a global optimum.

### Methods of Proof
There are two primary ways to mathematically or logically prove a greedy algorithm:
1. **Exchange Argument**: Assume there is an optimal solution that differs from the greedy solution. Show that you can swap elements to make the optimal solution look like the greedy solution without worsening its cost or validity.
2. **Greedy Stays Ahead**: Show that after every step, the greedy solution is at least as far along (or "as good") as any other valid solution.

By understanding these proof techniques, you can build a strong intuition for when to apply greedy algorithms versus when to rely on dynamic programming.

# Common Pitfalls
<Warning>
- Trusting intuition without finding a counter-example.
- Confusing a greedy problem with a 0/1 Knapsack style Dynamic Programming problem.
- Over-complicating the proof. During an interview, an informal exchange argument is usually sufficient.
</Warning>
