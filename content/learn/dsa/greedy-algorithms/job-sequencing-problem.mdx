# Theory & Concepts
The **Job Sequencing Problem** is a scheduling puzzle where you are given a set of jobs. Each job takes exactly 1 unit of time to complete, has a specific deadline, and yields a specific profit if completed before or exactly on its deadline. Your objective is to maximize the total profit by scheduling the jobs optimally.

### The Greedy Strategy
Since our goal is to maximize profit, the greedy choice is obvious: **always prioritize the jobs with the highest profit**. 
However, just picking high-profit jobs isn't enough; we must also schedule them smartly so they don't block other jobs. To leave room for as many jobs as possible, we should schedule a job **as close to its deadline as possible**.

1. **Sort** all jobs in descending order based on their profit.
2. Find the maximum deadline among all jobs to determine the size of our time schedule array.
3. Create an array (or boolean slots) representing time slots, initialized as empty.
4. Iterate through the sorted jobs. For each job, start checking time slots backwards from its deadline down to day 1. 
5. Assign the job to the first available empty slot found. If no slot is available, the job cannot be completed.

class Solution:

# Common Pitfalls
<Warning>
- Scheduling jobs at the *earliest* available slot instead of the *latest*. Scheduling early blocks time slots that could be used by jobs with tighter deadlines!
- Ignoring 1-based indexing for days. Day 1 starts at index 1 in the slots array, not index 0.
</Warning>
