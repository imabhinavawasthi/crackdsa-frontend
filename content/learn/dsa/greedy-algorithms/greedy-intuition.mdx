# Theory & Concepts
The **Greedy Choice Property** is the fundamental principle behind all Greedy Algorithms. It states that a globally optimal solution can be arrived at by making a locally optimal choice at each step. In other words, you never need to look back or reconsider your previous choices.

When a problem exhibits the Greedy Choice Property, it means that instead of exhaustively searching all possible paths (like in backtracking or dynamic programming), you can simply pick the "best" looking option right now and confidently move forward. The "best" option is usually defined by some heuristic, such as:
- The smallest/largest element.
- The earliest finishing time.
- The maximum immediate profit.

### Why does it work?
A greedy algorithm works if the problem has two key properties:
1. **Greedy Choice Property**: Making the locally best choice never prevents us from reaching the global optimum.
2. **Optimal Substructure**: The optimal solution to the overall problem contains the optimal solutions to its subproblems.

If a problem lacks the Greedy Choice Property, a greedy approach will yield a sub-optimal solution, and you must rely on Dynamic Programming.

# Common Pitfalls
<Warning>
- Assuming a problem has the greedy choice property without verifying. Many DP problems masquerade as Greedy ones!
- Not sorting the input. Greedy choices almost always rely on the input being sorted in a specific order (ascending, descending, by finish time, etc.).
- Failing to consider edge cases where the greedy choice locks you out of the only valid solution path.
</Warning>
