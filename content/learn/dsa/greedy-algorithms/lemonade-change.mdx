# Theory & Concepts
In the **Lemonade Change** problem, you are selling lemonade at $5 a cup. Customers stand in a queue and buy one cup at a time, paying with either a $5, $10, or $20 bill. You start with no change in your register. You must provide the correct change to every customer, serving them in the order they appear.

### The Greedy Strategy
This problem is a pure simulation combined with a greedy choice.
When a customer pays with:
- **$5 bill**: Keep it. No change needed.
- **$10 bill**: You must give exactly one $5 bill as change. (Keep the $10).
- **$20 bill**: You need to give $15 in change. Here is where the Greedy Choice comes in: you can either give three $5 bills, or one $10 bill and one $5 bill. 

**The Greedy Choice**: Always give the $10 bill + $5 bill if possible! Why? Because $5 bills are strictly more versatile. You need $5 bills to give change for $10s, and they can also be used to give change for $20s. A $10 bill is only useful for making change for a $20. By hoarding your $5 bills, you maximize your chances of making change for future customers.

# Common Pitfalls
<Warning>
- Using up three $5 bills to make change for a $20 when you have a $10 bill available. This violates the greedy principle and will cause you to fail subsequent test cases!
- Attempting to sort the input array. The customers are in a queue and must be served in the order they arrive. Sorting destroys the sequence of events.
</Warning>
