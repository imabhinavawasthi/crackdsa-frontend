# Theory & Concepts

**Trapping Rain Water** is legendary. While the Two-Pointer approach is the most space-efficient, the **Monotonic Stack** approach is arguably the most intuitive when you think about the geometry of the problem.

**Intuition**
Water is trapped when a shorter bar is sandwiched between two taller bars. This forms a "basin" or "valley".
We can use a **Monotonic Decreasing Stack** to track bars. As long as the bars are decreasing, no water is trapped (it just runs off the side). But the moment we encounter a bar that is *taller* than the top of our stack, a valley is formed!

When a valley is formed:
1. The `bottom` of the valley is the popped element.
2. The `left_boundary` is the new top of the stack.
3. The `right_boundary` is our current element.
The water trapped at this specific level is `(min(left_height, right_height) - bottom_height) * width`. We add this to our total and repeat.

            }
            st.push(i);
        }

# Common Pitfalls

<Warning>
- **No Left Boundary:** When popping the bottom of a valley, if the stack becomes empty, there is no left wall to hold the water. You must `break` out of the loop!
- **Calculating Height incorrectly:** The height of the trapped water is determined by the minimum of the two boundaries minus the height of the valley floor: `min(left_bound, right_bound) - bottom`.
</Warning>
